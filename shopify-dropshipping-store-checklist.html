Shopify Store Setup Checklist for Dropshipping (2027)

Everything to set up before you spend a dollar on ads. Work through it top to bottom and you will launch with a store that loads fast, earns trust and does not leak buyers at checkout.

By ChimeRaid Ecom. Updated October 2026. About 6 minutes to read.

In this checklist

  1. Before you build anything
  2. Theme, speed and mobile
  3. The product page
  4. Trust and policies
  5. Checkout and payments
  6. Tracking and analytics
  7. Supplier and fulfilment
  8. Launch-day test run
  9. FAQ

A store does not need to be fancy to sell. It needs to be fast, clear and trustworthy, and it needs a checkout that works on the first try. Most new stores lose money on the basics, not the design.

Use this as a list to tick off. Each section explains why the step matters, so you know what to fix if your numbers come in low later.

1. Before you build anything

  • Pick a product that passed research. Demand, margin, velocity and a clear angle. If you have not done this yet, start with our guide on how to find winning dropshipping products.
  • Write down your break-even CPA. Selling price minus product cost, shipping and payment fees. Every decision on this list should protect that number.
  • Get a short, simple domain. Easy to say, easy to spell, no hyphens or numbers.
  • Set up a business email on your domain for customer support. It looks far more trustworthy than a free email address.

2. Theme, speed and mobile

Most of your traffic will come from phones, through ads, with people who give you a few seconds before they scroll away. Slow pages waste the ad money you paid to get them there.

  • Choose one clean, fast theme. A free theme is fine for testing. Do not pay for a premium theme until a product proves it sells.
  • Aim for a page that loads in under 3 seconds on mobile. Test it with Google PageSpeed Insights.
  • Compress every image before uploading. Large photos are the most common cause of a slow store.
  • Keep apps to a minimum. Each one can add scripts that slow the page. Remove anything you are not actively using.
  • Check every page on your own phone, not only on a desktop preview.

3. The product page

This is where the sale happens. A visitor should understand what the product is, who it is for and why they want it without scrolling.

  • A benefit-led headline, not only the product name. Say what it does for the buyer.
  • Real photos and a short video of the product in use. Show it solving the problem.
  • Three to five clear benefits near the top, written for the buyer your ad targets.
  • Honest delivery times on the page. Surprises about shipping are a top reason for chargebacks.
  • Only real reviews. Fake reviews can break platform rules and destroy trust if a buyer spots them.
  • A clear price with no hidden fees showing up at checkout.
  • An FAQ section that answers the questions people ask in your ad comments.

4. Trust and policies

People are buying from a store they have never heard of. Every trust signal you add lowers the risk they feel.

  • Shipping policy with real processing and delivery times.
  • Refund and return policy in plain language.
  • Privacy policy and terms of service.
  • A contact page with your support email and a promised response time.
  • An about section that explains who you are and why the store exists.
  • Links to all policies in the footer, where buyers expect to find them.

5. Checkout and payments

Everyone who reaches checkout already decided to buy. Losing them here is the most expensive mistake on this list.

  • Turn on express checkout options such as Shop Pay, PayPal, Apple Pay and Google Pay where they are available to you. Fewer fields means more completed orders.
  • Set shipping rates before launch and show them on the product page, so the total never shocks anyone.
  • Remove distractions from the cart, like extra pop-ups and long upsell chains, until you have data showing they help.
  • Place a real test order from your phone, start to finish, and refund it. Check the confirmation email too.

6. Tracking and analytics

Without tracking, you cannot tell which ads make money. That turns scaling into guessing.

  • Install your ad platform's pixel (for example Meta or TikTok) through its official Shopify app, and confirm it records page views, add to carts and purchases.
  • Use UTM links on every ad, so you can see which campaigns bring sales in your Shopify reports.
  • Know your key numbers before you run ads: click-through rate, add-to-cart rate, checkout started and cost per purchase.

Our KPI gates: click-through above 1.5%, add-to-cart above 4% and checkout started above 2%. If you miss one, the gap tells you which part of this checklist to revisit.

7. Supplier and fulfilment

  • Order the product yourself first and check quality, packaging and real delivery time.
  • Agree on processing times with your supplier and write them down.
  • Have a backup supplier lined up in case stock runs out mid-campaign.
  • Make sure tracking numbers sync to orders automatically, so customers get shipping updates without asking.

8. Launch-day test run

Before you switch on ads, go through the store as if you were a customer.

  1. Open your store from an ad-style link on your phone and time how long it takes to load.
  2. Read the product page top to bottom. Does every claim make sense and hold up?
  3. Add to cart, go through checkout with an express payment option and complete the order.
  4. Check that your pixel recorded the purchase and that the order shows up in Shopify.
  5. Click every footer link, including all policy pages, and confirm none are broken.
  6. Send a test question to your support email and confirm it arrives.

If everything passes, you are ready for your 72-hour validation loop.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to set up a Shopify dropshipping store?

A focused build takes a few days to a week if your product is already chosen. Most of that time goes into the product page, creatives and testing checkout, not the theme. Rushing past the checklist is what causes refunds and wasted ad spend later.

Do I need a paid Shopify theme?

No. A clean, fast free theme is enough to launch and test. What matters more is page speed, a clear product page and a smooth checkout. Upgrade the theme later, once a product has proven it sells.

How many apps should a new dropshipping store have?

As few as possible. Every app can add scripts that slow your store down. Start with what you need to sell, fulfil and track orders, and add more only when you have a specific problem to solve.
